      Under the hood

      Meaning averages word vectors with a lower weight for stop words. Nearby words choose the sense of “python” and “bat”. Unknown words use signed, hashed character n-grams. Surface wording compares character fragments of the whole sentence. PCA uses power iteration on centered vectors.       Rows ask, columns supply context. A brighter cell carries more weight.

      Inside the selected cell

      Projected vectors

      Why this matters. Attention allows each token to combine information from different positions. In a causal model, it can only read its own position and earlier ones.

      Under the hood

      These teaching weights come from a toy setup, not a trained LLM’s attention. Each byte-pair token gets a 16-dimensional teaching vector and a small sinusoidal position signal. Three fixed, seeded matrices project it into 8-dimensional queries, keys and values. A stable row-wise softmax turns scaled scores into weights that sum to one. The output is a weighted sum of value vectors.

      Why this matters. A probability distribution can yield different continuations. Sampling settings determine how often rare choices appear.

      Under the hood

      Each nonempty corpus line is a training sequence with start and end markers. A trigram uses the previous two tokens. Unseen contexts back off to one token, then to corpus-wide counts. Sampling applies temperature to log counts, keeps the top k choices, then keeps the smallest prefix reaching top-p of the remaining probability. The survivors are renormalized. Generation stops at an end marker or 24 sampled tokens.

      LLM Pipeline Explorer 2

      About this lab

      LLM Pipeline Explorer 2 is a single-file, offline experiment. The four stages let you inspect the arithmetic behind common language-model operations.

      What the stages teach

      Tokenizer. Train real byte-pair encoding on a corpus. The base vocabulary has 256 byte IDs plus two reserved markers. The slider chooses a prefix of the learned merge list. Replay shows the winning pair at each step. Change the corpus and choose Build vocabulary to apply edits.

      Embeddings. Compare hand-written word entries across 16 feature dimensions, or switch to a 64-dimensional hashed spelling representation. Colors come from deterministic k-means. Hover over a point or select its ID to inspect the full sentence. Neighbor scores and the heatmap use cosine similarity before 3D projection.

      Attention. The encoder and attention share a sentence. Select a cell to inspect query–key multiplication, scaling, softmax and the weighted sum of values. Removing the causal mask lets tokens attend in both directions.

      Next token. The sampler uses the tokenizer’s active BPE vocabulary on the applied corpus. It learns counts per line. Adjust the order, temperature and filters to explore alternative continuations. Sampling uses fresh randomness on each draw.

      Design choices and limits

      These are connected teaching experiments. The embeddings are hand-assigned, the attention projections are seeded random weights, and the predictor is a separate count model. The predictor uses corpus statistics independently of the attention output.

      BPE segments text into word-like runs, numbers, punctuation and whitespace. A leading space can join the next word. Ties use the first pair observed. Training stops after 144 merges or when no adjacent pair occurs twice. Raw character counts use Unicode code points. Chars per token is a sequence-length ratio. JSON exports include original bytes for lossless decoding. Sequence markers are excluded from encoder counts. The word baseline ignores whitespace and counts punctuation separately.

      The English teaching lexicon groups related words into shared feature profiles. Five words on either side provide sense context. Ties choose the animal sense. Unknown words, punctuation and other languages fall back to character fragments. Hash collisions and shared feature profiles can produce close or overlapping points. PCA retains the first three directions, and k-means can find fewer distinct centers than requested.

      To keep interaction quick, the lab accepts 5,000 corpus characters, 400 per encoder sentence or generation prompt, and 24 plot points of up to 160 characters. Attention displays the first 24 tokens. Generation stops at 24 samples or an end marker. An incomplete UTF-8 byte sequence may appear as a pending fragment during generation.
